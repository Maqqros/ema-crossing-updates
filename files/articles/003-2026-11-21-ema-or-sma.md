# EMA or SMA? A coin toss, share by share, and again half by half

Date: 2026-11-21

On 3,273 US shares before 2022, the 50/200 crossing rule gave the typical share about 2.0% a year with either kind of average, each kind did better in about half the shares, and the kind that did better in the first half of a share's history did better in the second half only 49% of the time.

## Two averages, one rule

A simple moving average (SMA) weighs the last 50 or 200 closing prices equally. An exponential one (EMA) gives more weight to the most recent prices, so it turns sooner. Market Lab ran the same rule with both: buy when the 50-day average crosses above the 200-day average, sell when it crosses back below, 0.20% cost on each buy and sell, starting invested on the first day. Both versions were tested over exactly the same days on 3,273 US shares listed today, with prices from 1962 to the end of 2021. No order was placed.

## Nearly the same days

On the typical share, the two versions were in the same position, both holding or both in cash, on 92% of the days.

Where both bought within 60 trading days of each other (23,305 times), the EMA version bought first in 72% of cases, by a **median** of 9 trading days. The median is the middle value when all are lined up. Sales were closer together: a median of 3 trading days.

## The same result for the typical share

The typical share gained 1.97% a year with EMA and 1.96% with SMA, against 7.4% for simply holding. EMA ended ahead of holding in 962 shares (29%), SMA in 977 (30%).

The trades looked different, though. SMA made 31,750 trades, 45% of them winners, with a median trade of -2.3%. EMA made 30,385, 39% winners, median -5.1%. EMA's mean trade was larger, 15.6% against 13.6%, and it all came out about even.

## A coin toss, share by share

- EMA did better in 1,625 shares (49.6%).
- SMA did better in 1,581 (48.3%).
- They tied in 67.

The gaps were not small. In 1,572 shares (48%), the two versions ended at least 2 percentage points a year apart, and in 678 (21%) at least 5 points apart. Only 32% (1,032) ended within 1 point a year of each other.

## Did the better one stay better?

Market Lab split each share's history into two halves of equal length and tested each half on its own. In 2,689 shares, one version did better in each half. The same version did better in both halves in 1,317 of them, 49%.

Even where the first half's gap was 2 points a year or more (1,481 shares), the same version did better again in the second half in 721, 48.7%.

## Thai shares and US funds

- Thai shares, 1987 to 2021: SMA did better in 282 of 514 (55%), EMA in 216 (42%). The same version did better in both halves in 148 of 306 shares (48%).
- US ETFs, 1993 to 2021: EMA did better in 622 of 1,268 (49%), SMA in 548 (43%), 98 tied. The same version did better in both halves in 440 of 897 (49%).

## The limits

- **Missing losers.** Yahoo removes companies that went bankrupt or were delisted. Only today's survivors were tested.
- **Hypothetical.** No order was placed. Real trading meets spreads, taxes and orders that do not fill.
- **Halves are short.** Each half is tested on its own and starts invested again, so its results are noisier than the whole.
- **Many rules, many symbols.** This article compares two rules on thousands of shares. Some differences arise by luck alone.
- **2022 onward was held back** and has not been evaluated.

Nothing here is a forecast or a suggestion to buy or sell.
