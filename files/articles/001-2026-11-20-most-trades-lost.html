Six in ten trades lost money. The best one in thirteen carried the average.

On 3,273 US shares before 2022, a rule that traded the EMA 50 crossing the EMA 200 lost money on 61% of its 30,385 trades, yet its average trade gained 15.6%, because a small number of very large winners outweighed all the rest.

50%

What was tested

Market Lab ran one simple rule on daily prices: buy when the 50-day EMA crosses above the 200-day EMA, sell when it crosses back below. Each buy and each sell was charged 0.20%. The test used 3,273 US shares that are listed today, with prices from 1962 to the end of 2021. No real order was placed.

Mostly small losses

Of the 30,385 trades, 18,659 ended at a loss. Only 39% were winners.

The median trade, meaning the middle one when all trades are lined up from worst to best, lost 5.1%. The mean, the ordinary average, was a gain of 15.6%. The median shows what a typical trade looked like. The mean is pulled up by a few huge results.

The losses were mostly small and quick. The middle losing trade lost 10.7% and lasted 97 days. About half of all trades ended between zero and a 20% loss.

A few long winners

The winners looked very different. The middle winning trade gained 29.7% and lasted 517 days, more than five times as long as the middle loser.

The best trades did the work

Put plainly, the rule was wrong most of the time and stayed in for a long time on the rare occasions it was very right. On US shares:

  • The best 5% of trades (1,519 trades, each gaining more than 114%) lifted the average from 2.7% to 15.6%.
  • Without the best 2,333 trades, about one in thirteen, the average of all the others was zero or below.
  • 125 trades, fewer than one in 200, gained more than 500% each.

Deal yourself ten trades

Each deal draws ten trades at random from a sample of the rule's real trades. Most deals are mostly losers. Now and then a deal catches a huge winner, and its average jumps.

    Thai shares showed the same shape

    On 514 Thai shares, with prices from 1987 to the end of 2021, the rule made 1,704 trades. 38% were winners. The median trade lost 5.9% and the mean trade gained 21.3%. Without the best 5% (85 trades), the average of the rest was 2.7%. Every chart here has a Thai shares button, and a US ETFs one too.

    Even so, holding usually ended ahead

    A positive average trade did not mean the rule beat doing nothing. Over the same days, the rule ended ahead of simply holding the same share in only 29% of the US shares (962 of 3,273) and 37% of the Thai shares (189 of 514). For the typical US share the rule gained 2.0% a year, against 7.4% for holding.

    Share by share: the rule against holding

    For each share, the rule and simply holding were measured over exactly the same days.

    What the rule did change was the depth of the falls. Its worst fall from an earlier peak was smaller than holding's in 81% of the US shares and 91% of the Thai shares. An equal mix of the US shares fell at most 23% with the rule, against 54% for holding, between 1969 and 2021.

    A faster rule traded far more and gained less per trade

    An earlier Market Lab run compared eight rules on US shares before 2022. In that run each rule waited for its first cross before buying, so its figures cannot be mixed with those above. There, price crossing the EMA 50 made 433,034 trades, held for a median of 6 days. A quarter were winners and the average trade gained 0.4%. EMA 50 crossing EMA 200 made 27,708 trades in the same run, with an average of 15.2%.

    The limits

    • Missing losers. Yahoo removes companies that went bankrupt or were delisted. Only today's survivors were tested, so both the rule and holding look better here than they really were.
    • Hypothetical. No order was placed. Real trading meets spreads, taxes and orders that do not fill.
    • An average trade is not a yearly return. Trades differ in length. The count includes each share's first purchase on the first day of its test, and 1,642 trades still open at the end of 2021, valued at the last price.
    • Thai shares sat mostly in cash. The typical Thai share was held only 16% of the time, against 57% for the typical US share. That alone makes falls smaller.
    • The equal mix is a measuring device. It is rebalanced every day at no cost, which no real account could do.
    • Many rules, many symbols. Some results look good by luck.
    • 2022 onward was held back and has not been evaluated.

    Nothing here is a forecast or a suggestion to buy or sell.
