# 8 times in 10, the crossover rule sold, then paid more to buy back in

Date: 2026-11-21

On 4,268 US shares from the start of 2021 to November 2026, the EMA 50/200 rule sold and later bought back 11,806 times, and in 80% of them it paid more to get back in than it had received, yet added up over all shares it came out ahead of holding, because many shares it sold kept falling.

## Where a difference comes from

The rule buys when the 50-day EMA crosses above the 200-day EMA and sells when it crosses back below, paying 0.20% on each buy and sell. It bought each share on the first day, like holding. Only trading from 1 January 2021 to 17 November 2026 was counted.

Market Lab split the difference between the rule and holding into exact parts. Each time the rule sold and later bought back, it either bought back higher, which is growth it missed, or lower, which is a fall it avoided. The other parts are price moves after its last sale, for shares not bought back by the end, and trading costs.

## The price usually dipped, then rose past the sale price

In 87% of the 11,806 spells in cash (10,232), the price fell below the sale price at some point. The **median** lowest point, meaning the middle one when all are lined up, was 8.7% below the sale price.

But the rule only buys back once the 50-day average climbs back above the 200-day average, and by then the price had usually recovered. It bought back higher than it sold in 80% of the spells (9,484). The median buy-back price was 10.2% above the sale price and 26.7% above the lowest close in between.

## Short spells were nearly always dearer

- Spells in cash of up to 120 days: 6,306, and 99.7% ended with buying back higher.
- Spells of more than 250 days: 3,188, and only 31% ended higher.

The long spells covered the slow declines the rule is meant to sidestep. The short ones were false alarms, and there were more of them.

## Adding up the difference

Over all 4,268 shares, the parts were added on a logarithmic scale, which lets them sum exactly. If the cost of the dearer buy-backs is counted as 100:

- falls avoided by buying back lower won back 64,
- price moves after the rule's last sale won back 157,
- trading costs added 3.

So, added up, the rule ended ahead of holding by 118 on that scale. The big piece is the last sale: 2,460 of the shares were still sold on 17 November 2026, and many had fallen far since.

This sum gives great weight to shares that collapsed. Share by share the picture is different: in 57% of the shares (2,432) the dearer buy-backs outweighed the avoided falls, and the rule ended ahead of holding in only 1,816 (43%). The typical share had 4 trades, and costs took about 1.6%.

## Two examples

Coca-Cola (KO): the rule sold and bought back 4 times, all higher. Holding grew 2.23-fold, the rule 1.53-fold.

The Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ: the rule sold in March 2022 and bought back in March 2023, 7.8% lower than it sold. Its other buy-back, in May 2025, was 15.7% higher. It ended 2.04-fold against 2.19-fold for holding.

## Thai shares and US funds

- Thai shares: 543 spells, 70% bought back higher (382). Many Thai shares fell and stayed down after the rule sold, so added up the rule ended far ahead of holding.
- US ETFs: 3,636 spells, 82% higher (2,980). Here holding ended ahead overall.

## The limits

- **Missing losers.** Yahoo removes companies that went bankrupt or were delisted. Many fell hard before they vanished, which is where selling helps most.
- **Hypothetical.** No order was placed. Real trading meets spreads, taxes and orders that do not fill.
- **One way of adding up.** The parts sum exactly only on a logarithmic scale, which weighs collapses heavily. Measured another way, their sizes would differ.
- **A short period.** Under six years, and the last sale is judged at one date. Nothing was held back for a later check.
- **Chosen examples.** The five example symbols were picked because they are well known, not at random.
- **Faulty prices left out.** 103 symbols with jumps that look like unadjusted splits were dropped.

Nothing here is a forecast or a suggestion to buy or sell.
