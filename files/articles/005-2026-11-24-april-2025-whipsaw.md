# The April 2025 trap: sold in the drop, bought back higher 94 times in 100

Date: 2026-11-24

In March and April 2025, the EMA 50/200 rule sold 922 of the 1,997 US shares it held, 42% of its sales came after the market's low of 8 April, and 94% of the buy-backs that followed cost more than the sale had brought in, a median 16% more.

## A quick fall and a quick recovery

Article 004 found that sales made in the second quarter of 2025 cost the rule more than those of any other quarter. This article looks at them closely.

An equal mix of the US shares tested fell 24.0% from 2 December 2024 to 8 April 2025, its lowest close of the year. By 10 July it was back at its earlier high.

The rule is the same as in articles 001 to 004: buy when the 50-day EMA crosses above the 200-day EMA, sell when it crosses back below, 0.20% cost on each buy and sell. No order was placed.

## The rule sold late

At the close of 28 February 2025 the rule held 1,997 US shares. By the end of April it had sold 922 of them, 46%. In all it made 976 sales in March and April, 252 of them in the week of 7 to 11 April alone.

409 sales, 42%, came after the low of 8 April. The 50-day average falls slowly, so it often crossed below the 200-day average only once the price had already turned up.

## And bought back later, and higher

By 17 November 2026, 911 of the 976 sales had been bought back. 856 of those buy-backs, 94%, were at a higher price than the sale. The **median** buy-back, meaning the middle one when all are lined up, was 16.1% above the sale price, after a median of 90 days in cash.

The price did dip below the sale price after 78% of the sales, but not by much: the median lowest point was 6.9% below. The median buy-back came 27.9% above that lowest close. Fifty trading days after the sale, the median price already stood 11.2% above the sale price, and only 31% of the sales had been bought back by the end of May.

Sales made after the low were bought back higher even more often: 97%, after a median of 50 days. For sales on or before the low: 91%, after 114 days.

## Six well-known examples

- SPY, the S&P 500 fund, was sold on 17 April and bought back on 16 May, 12.1% higher.
- NVDA was sold on 8 April, the day of the low, and bought back on 22 May, 27.4% higher.
- TSLA was sold on 9 April and bought back on 19 May, 49.7% higher.
- QQQ, AAPL and MSFT were bought back 15.7%, 34.1% and 16.0% higher.

## Few sales paid

Only 105 of the 976 sales, 11%, paid: the rule bought back lower, or the price still stood below the sale price on 17 November 2026. 65 shares were never bought back, and their median price stood 47.5% below the sale price.

## The slow fall of 2022 was different

For comparison, in April to June 2022 the rule sold 948 times. 74% of those buy-backs were higher, a median of 8.9%, after 190 days in cash. Fifty trading days after a sale, the median price stood 0.4% below the sale price.

## US funds

The rule held 1,302 US ETFs at the end of February and sold 624 of them, 48%. Of 668 sales in March and April, 60% came after the low. 574 of the 578 buy-backs, 99%, were higher, a median of 10.5%, after 52 days.

## The limits

- **One episode, picked after the fact.** This drop was chosen because article 004 found it costly. Other quick falls might look different.
- **Missing losers.** Yahoo removes companies that went bankrupt or were delisted.
- **Hypothetical.** No order was placed. Real trading meets spreads, taxes and orders that do not fill.
- **Adjusted prices.** Prices include dividends, so they differ from the quoted ones.
- **Judged at one date.** A sale not bought back is valued at the close of 17 November 2026.
- **A short period.** Trading counted from 2021 only, with nothing held back for a later check. 103 symbols with faulty prices were left out.

Nothing here is a forecast or a suggestion to buy or sell.
