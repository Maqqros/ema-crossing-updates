# Cut your losses and sit in cash? It lost to doing nothing 84 times in 100

Date: 2026-11-24

On 2,599 US shares bought at random on the first trading day of 2021, selling a share once it fell 20% below its peak and keeping the cash left a 10-share portfolio behind simply holding 84 times in 100 by November 2026, while buying another random share after each sale gave a median of +64% against +61% for holding.

## The test

Market Lab took every US share that was listed and actively traded on 4 January 2021 and bought it at that day's opening price. Then it followed two plans to 17 November 2026:

- **Hold:** keep the share.
- **Cut at X%:** sell at the next opening price once the share closes X% or more below its highest close since buying, for X from 5% to 60%.

Each buy and sell cost 0.20%. Random portfolios of 10 shares were drawn 20,000 times. No order was placed.

## Nearly every share fell 20% at some point

99% of the shares fell at least 20% below their peak while held. The **median**, meaning the middle share when all are lined up, fell 57% from its peak at its worst.

Held to the end, the median share gained 30.6%. 38% lost money, 22% lost more than half, and 29% more than doubled.

## Cut and stay out

With a 20% cut, 99% of the shares were sold, after a median of 220 days, and the cash then earned nothing. Share by share, no cut level ended ahead of holding in more than 41% of the shares.

For random 10-share portfolios:

- Holding: median +61.1%; 6.6% of portfolios lost money.
- Cut at 20%: median +16.1%; 2.3% lost money; ahead of holding the same ten shares in 16% of portfolios.
- Cut at 50%: median +46.7%; ahead in 38%.

The cut saved some shares and cost others. PayPal (PYPL), held, ended at a quarter of its price; cut at 20% in March 2021, it ended 1.8% up. NVIDIA (NVDA), cut at 20% the same month, ended at 0.92 of its price, while holding grew it nearly twelvefold.

## Cut and pick again

In a second version, the money from each sale bought another share drawn at random that same morning, which then followed the same rule. Over 2,000 portfolios of 10:

- Cut at 20%: median +63.9%, with a median of 7 sales per slot; 1.6% of portfolios lost money. Nine portfolios in ten gained more than 20.7%, against more than 7.9% when holding.
- Cut at 30%: median +68.2%, 3 sales per slot; 1.1% lost money.
- Cut at 10%: median +41.5%. The rule sold a median of 29 times per slot, and costs added up.

## Thai shares went the other way

The 182 Thai shares tested fell over the period: the median share lost 12% held. Cutting at 25% and staying out beat holding the same ten shares in 84% of random portfolios. Cutting at 30% and picking again gave a median of +31% against +10% for holding.

## The limits

- **Missing losers.** Yahoo removes companies that went bankrupt or were delisted. Every share here survived, which flatters holding, and every new pick in the second version was a survivor too.
- **One start date.** All shares were bought on 4 January 2021, a year before a broad decline. Another start would give other numbers.
- **Cash earned nothing.** Real cash earned interest in 2023 to 2025.
- **Hypothetical.** No order was placed. Real trading meets spreads, taxes and orders that do not fill.
- **A short period.** Under six years, with nothing held back for a later check. 103 symbols with faulty prices were left out.

Nothing here is a forecast or a suggestion to buy or sell.
