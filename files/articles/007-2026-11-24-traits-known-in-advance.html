The beaten-down shares lost 42%. The ones near their high made 71%

Sorting about 2,950 liquid US shares into fifths each month from January 2021 to October 2026, the fifth furthest below its 52-week high lost 42% over the period while the fifth closest to it gained 71%, against 38% for all of them, and for six of seven traits the worst fifth fell further behind the average than the best got ahead.

Before you read: guess

Every month from January 2021, the fifth of US shares furthest below their highest price of the past year was bought, an equal amount in each, and swapped for the new fifth a month later. All shares together grew 38% by October 2026. What did this fifth do?

+0%

The test

Article 006 picked shares at random. This one sorts them by traits that anyone could see on the day.

At the end of each month, Market Lab split the actively traded US shares into five equal groups, called fifths, by one trait at a time. Each fifth was then held, an equal amount in every share, for the following month, from its first opening price to its last close. Then the shares were sorted again. The seven traits are the standard ones from finance research:

  • the past 12-month return, leaving out the latest month;
  • last month's return;
  • how much the price swung over the last year (volatility);
  • dividends paid over the last year, against the price (yield);
  • the money traded each day, a measure of size;
  • how close the price was to its highest close of the last year (the 52-week high);
  • the largest one-day jump last month.

Results run from January 2021 to October 2026; November 2026 was not finished. Company profits are not in Market Lab's data, so they were not tested. No order was placed.

The worst fifths stood out most

All shares together, an equal amount in each, grew 33% to 40% over the period, depending on which shares had the trait measured. Against that:

  • Furthest below the 52-week high: lost 42%. Closest to it: gained 71%.
  • Most volatile: lost 30%. Least volatile: gained 47%.
  • Biggest one-day jump last month: lost 22%.
  • Worst past 12-month return: lost 13%. Best: gained 40%, about the same as all shares.
  • Highest dividend yield: gained 71%. Lowest: 5%.
  • Biggest by money traded: gained 64%. Smallest: 10%.

Only for size did the best fifth get further ahead of the average than the worst fell behind it.

Steady or lucky?

A common check is the t-value, which asks whether a gap between two groups was steady enough from month to month to be told apart from luck. A value of 2 or more is the usual bar. For US shares, none of the seven gaps between the highest and lowest fifth reached it; the largest was 1.7, for size.

Year by year, the biggest shares beat the smallest in all six years, and the fifth nearest its high beat the fifth furthest below it in five.

Keeping a fifth up to date

The fifths change every month. In the fifth closest to its 52-week high, 42% of the shares were new each month. At 0.20% for each buy and sell, keeping it up to date would have cost about 2% a year, which the figures above leave out. For last month's best performers, 77% were new each month, about 3.7% a year.

Thai shares

Thai shares as a whole lost about 12%. The fifth closest to its 52-week high gained 43% and beat the fifth furthest below it, which lost 62%, in all six years. The best past 12-month return (+40%) and the highest dividend yield (+43%) also stood apart. These three gaps reached a t-value above 2, though each fifth held only about 35 shares. The limits

  • Missing losers. Yahoo removes companies that went bankrupt or were delisted. They would mostly have sat in the weak fifths, so those fifths look better here than they were.
  • Many traits, two markets. Fourteen comparisons were made; some gaps look large by luck alone.
  • Hypothetical. No order was placed. Real trading meets spreads, taxes and orders that do not fill.
  • Equal amounts, rebalanced monthly. No real account could do this without further costs.
  • A short period. Under six years, with nothing held back for a later check. 103 symbols with faulty prices were left out.

Nothing here is a forecast or a suggestion to buy or sell.
